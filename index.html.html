Curator’s Note

Mahira Ali
WAHID — واحد
One Spirit – Shared Heritage – Diverse Expressions

Art has a unique ability to bring people together beyond the boundaries of language, nationality, and individual experience. It creates a space where different perspectives can coexist, where identities can be expressed freely, and where shared human values emerge through colour, form, material, and imagination.

WAHID — واحد is conceived as a celebration of the artistic dialogue between Saudi Arabia and Pakistan. Bringing together artists from both countries, the exhibition explores the connections that emerge through shared heritage, cultural memory, human experience, and diverse creative expressions. Each artist contributes a distinct visual language, shaped by personal journeys, cultural influences, and individual ways of seeing the world.

The concept of Wahid, meaning “One,” speaks to unity without requiring uniformity. It invites us to recognise that our differences are not barriers to understanding, but opportunities for meaningful exchange. Through contemporary painting, calligraphy, and varied artistic practices, the exhibition creates a meeting point where distinct voices retain their individuality while participating in a broader cultural conversation.

As curator, my intention is to create a balanced and inclusive platform that honours the artistic identities of both nations and encourages dialogue, appreciation, and lasting cultural connections. I believe that art becomes most meaningful when it allows us not only to see another perspective, but also to discover something of ourselves within it.

WAHID is, ultimately, an invitation to encounter diversity with openness, to recognise the beauty of shared human experience, and to celebrate the many expressions through which unity can be imagined and experienced.

Mahira Ali
Curator, WAHID — واحد

H.E. Ahmad Farooq

Ambassador

Biography

H.E. Ahmad Farooq is the Ambassador of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan to the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and a painter whose artistic practice reflects a deep appreciation for history, architecture, and cultural heritage. Alongside his diplomatic responsibilities, he explores the historic city of Diriyah through paintings that capture its earthen walls, traditional Najdi architecture, carved doors, courtyards, and everyday life.

His exhibition, *Diriyah: Past & Present — Diriyah Through the Eyes of an Ambassador*, brings together a collection of works celebrating Diriyah's historical significance and its continuing evolution. Through architectural studies, atmospheric evening scenes, and explorations of colour and light, Farooq pays tribute to Saudi heritage while highlighting the enduring friendship between Pakistan and Saudi Arabia.

Artist Statement

Through my paintings, I explore Diriyah as a place where history, architecture, and contemporary life exist in harmony. I am drawn to its earthen walls, distinctive Najdi patterns, historic palaces, shaded walkways, and the changing atmosphere of its streets from daylight to night.

My work seeks to capture more than the physical appearance of the city; it reflects the memory, identity, and cultural traditions embedded in its architecture. Through variations in light, colour, and perspective, I explore how a historic place can remain alive while embracing change.

*Diriyah: Past & Present* is my tribute to the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia's rich heritage and its remarkable journey into the future. It also expresses my appreciation for the longstanding friendship between Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, bringing together art, history, and cultural understanding on a shared canvas.

AbdulKhaleq Al-Zaid

Biography

Abdulkhaleq Al-Zaid is a Saudi visual artist whose practice explores the relationship between the Arabic letter and visual composition. Drawing inspiration from the aesthetics of Arabic calligraphy and its rich expressive energy, Al-Zaid focuses on the interlocking of letters and their abstract formations to present a contemporary vision that unites the authenticity of calligraphic traditions with the freedom of visual art. Working across diverse mediums—including wood—he leverages texture and visual depth to enrich the tactile experience of his art. Al-Zaid views the Arabic letter not merely as script, but as an open-ended plastic element capable of infinite possibilities that transcend the boundaries of writing into the realms of color, form, and texture.

Artist Statement

This artwork emerges from the overlapping of Arabic letters, transforming them into a visual composition where lines, masses, and spaces intertwine. Utilizing acrylic paints and wood carving, the piece highlights the relationship between the letterform and the material, granting the surface dimensions that transcend traditional color treatments. The contrast between warm yellow tones, shades of black, gray, and subtle traces of other colors creates a delicate balance between light and shadow, clarity and obscurity. The circular form of the artwork unifies the movement of the letters as they embrace and intertwine. Here, the letter is treated not merely as readable text, but as a visual element with its own unique rhythm and presence, offering the viewer an open space for contemplation and interpretation.

Al-Shehri’s contributions have been recognized with major awards, including First Place in the Islamic Ornamentation Competition (2002), the Abha Cultural Award for Fine Arts (2003 and 2017), the “Banat Al-Reeh” Arab Horse Competition First Prize (2011), the Abdullah Al-Qasabi Fine Arts Award (2014), and the Okaz “Painting and Poem” Prize (2014), with his artworks held in prestigious private and institutional collections throughout the region.

Artist Statement

I am a visual artist searching for meaning, moving between colors just as I move between cultures, searching for beauty in the details of life and translating it onto my canvases. I belong to my roots and take pride in my heritage, while simultaneously believing that openness to the world enriches my artistic and human experience. I balance between the passion of discovery and the tranquility of contemplation, carving out a journey for myself that unites authenticity with creativity and renewal.

Today, Bakhtawer continues to develop her own distinct visual language within Khat-e-Moalla, creating contemporary calligraphic compositions that honor tradition while expressing a unique artistic identity.

My practice is rooted in the spiritual and aesthetic traditions of Islamic calligraphy, with a particular focus on Khat-e-Moalla. Through precision, patience, and contemporary visual expression, I seek to honor the discipline of traditional calligraphy while creating works that invite viewers to experience the beauty of the Qur’anic word through seeing, feeling, and contemplation.

Amal Felemban

Visual Artist

Biography

Amal Hussein Felemban is a Saudi visual artist with a Bachelor’s degree in Islamic Arts and over 20 years of artistic experience. Her practice explores the relationship between people, place, memory, and cultural identity, drawing inspiration from the local environment, architectural heritage, and the distinctive details of the cities she has experienced.

She has participated in more than 60 local and international exhibitions and represented Saudi Arabia in Italy, Morocco, and France through nominations by the Ministry of Culture. Her achievements include numerous local and Gulf-region awards, as well as an international award. She has also presented a live solo exhibition on an NFT platform.

Felemban has created significant public artworks, including a 75-metre mural in Makkah and a 36-metre mural at the New King Abdulaziz International Airport, as well as a three-metre seagull sculpture on the Jeddah waterfront. Her works are held in collections belonging to government entities, ministries, residential developments, and private collectors. In addition to her artistic practice, she has worked as a visual arts instructor, delivered more than 50 workshops in Saudi Arabia and abroad, and contributed to the local arts community through professional committee memberships and the Jeddah Society for Culture and Arts.

Artist Statement

Person, Place and Memory

My artistic practice explores the relationship between people and place, and between memory and the traces that time leaves within us. Much of my visual vocabulary comes from the local environment, the details of the cities I have lived in and contemplated, and the heritage I see as a living part of our identity rather than merely a past to recall.

I lean towards reduction and abstraction, freeing elements from their literal reality to open new possibilities for interpretation. Colour, line, and texture are my essential tools, allowing me to construct emotional experiences rather than simply describe a particular scene.

Through my work, I seek the moment where past meets present, reality meets memory, and place meets person. I hope viewers discover fragments of their own memories within my work, sensing the stories and emotions that places leave behind.

For me, art is more than a visual practice; it is a means of preserving and reshaping memory, opening an ongoing dialogue with place, identity, and time.
